Spatial Analysis · Mar 2026

Identifying Service Deserts at the Intersection of Incarceration & Substance Use Risk

Do New York State counties with higher incarceration and overdose mortality have less geographic access to harm reduction services? Using county-level data from 2019 to 2023, I mapped overdose mortality, jail populations, and distance to the nearest syringe service or opioid treatment program in R, combining bivariate and trivariate choropleth maps with spatial statistics. I found that 40 of New York’s 62 counties have no identified syringe service or opioid treatment program, with the most critical service deserts concentrated in rural upstate communities.

Online Web Resource · Apr 2026

The “Policy as Medicine” Web Hub: A Social Justice Resource for Clinicians Servicing Immigrant Populations in New York City

As immigration enforcement shifts, clinicians caring for undocumented, mixed-status, and asylum-seeking patients need to know both their own obligations and their patients’ rights. With two colleagues, I co-created Policy as Medicine for a health messaging competition: a single web platform that compiles medical-legal resources for New York City clinicians, from ICE response protocols and patient protections under EMTALA and HIPAA to coverage programs, legal referrals, and a weekly policy tracker. The hub also offers downloadable pocket cards summarizing clinicians’ obligations, so providers can advocate for immigrant patients at the point of care.

Columbia Mailman, Lerner Center for Health Promotion

Applied Practice Experience · July 2026

Evaluating the Brooklyn Communities Collaborative (BCC) Maternal Health Program: A Community-Engaged Qualitative Study

How does the Brooklyn Communities Collaborative’s Maternal Health Program shape mothers’ health knowledge, access to care, and well-being? I mentored five high school researchers through a community-engaged qualitative study, guiding them from literature review through focus groups and key informant interviews to thematic analysis and dissemination. The team found that mothers valued continuous, community-based support over fragmented clinical care, while gaps remained in postpartum recovery, C-section care, and mental health support.
